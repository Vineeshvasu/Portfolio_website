Overview

The Challenge

Cardiologists in busy hospital settings were spending an average of 15+ minutes manually interpreting a single ECG. In emergency scenarios — STEMI, arrhythmias, life-threatening events — every minute matters.

Existing tools were designed for desktop, forcing doctors to move between devices mid-workflow. The goal was to design a mobile-first experience that puts AI-powered interpretation directly in the doctor's hands, at the point of care.

My Approach

How I Approached It

I treated the phone as a point-of-care instrument, not a smaller desktop. Every decision started from the moment a cardiologist has to act on an ECG and worked backwards from there.

The AI was designed as a second opinion the doctor stays in control of. It never acts as a black box: findings are explained, editable and always secondary to clinical judgement.

Vision & Innovation

The Vision

Move ECG interpretation off the desktop and into the cardiologist's pocket. The goal was AI-assisted reads at the bedside, in the corridor or on call, so that critical cases like STEMI reach a decision in minutes instead of waiting for a free workstation.

Identifying Unique Challenges

What Made It Hard

  • Dense data, small screen: A 12-lead ECG carries a lot of detail. It had to stay readable and comparable on a phone without endless zooming.
  • Trust in AI: Doctors won't act on a suggestion they can't verify. The interface had to show why the AI reached a finding, not just what it found.
  • Stress and one-handed use: Emergencies leave one hand free at best, so core actions had to work with a thumb, quickly and without mistakes.
  • Urgent vs routine: Life-threatening ECGs must stand out immediately from the routine queue.
Resolving Complex Problems

Design Solutions

Lead-focused waveform viewer

A clean lead-switching pattern lets doctors move between leads and focus on one without losing context. This removed the biggest bottleneck found in research.

Designed around the lead-switching bottleneck identified while shadowing 8 cardiologists.

Transparent AI panel

AI findings appear alongside the waveform they refer to, so doctors can verify, accept or edit each suggestion. Placement was refined over 4 rounds of doctor feedback.

AI trust signals tested and iterated across 4 design rounds.

Criticality-first triage

Critical findings rise to the top of the list with clear visual and text labels, so urgent cases never hide in a routine queue.

Triage scenarios used to benchmark against the previous desktop tool.

Thumb-zone actions

Primary actions sit within easy thumb reach, so a full read-and-report can be completed one-handed.

One-handed operation defined as a non-negotiable from day one.
User-Centric Design

Designed With Users

Cardiologists shaped the product at every stage, from contextual interviews and shadowing to moderated usability tests. Waveform clarity, typography and AI panel placement were all decided by watching real doctors use the prototype rather than by assumption.

Design Process

How We Got There

01 — Research

Shadowed 8 cardiologists across 3 hospitals. Ran contextual interviews to map their ECG interpretation workflow, pain points, and handoff friction.

Identified 3 critical bottlenecks: lead switching, AI trust signals, and one-handed operation under stress.

02 — Define

Synthesised findings into a core problem statement and user flows. Prioritised speed, legibility under stress, and one-handed operation as the three non-negotiables.

Defined 5 primary user flows mapped to clinical task sequences, validated with 3 cardiologists.

03 — Design

Rapid wireframing in FigJam, then high-fidelity prototypes in Figma. Iterated 4 rounds based on doctor feedback on waveform clarity and AI panel placement.

40+ screens across the core ECG interpretation, triage, and report flows.

04 — Test

Moderated usability tests with 12 doctors using Maze. Measured task completion time and error rate on triage scenarios against the previous desktop tool.

68% reduction in average interpretation time. 91% satisfaction score across tested users.
Accessibility & Optimisation

Built for Everyone

  • Legibility first: high-contrast waveform grids and type sized for quick reading under stress.
  • Not colour alone: criticality is shown with colour plus a text label, so it reads for colour-blind users too.
  • Platform conventions: built on iOS Human Interface Guidelines for familiar gestures, touch targets and Dynamic Type support.
  • Optimised flows: fewer taps from opening a case to signing off a report.
