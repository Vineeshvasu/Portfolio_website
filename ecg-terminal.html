Overview

The Challenge

Hospital networks needed a desktop-grade clinical platform for ECG analysis — one where doctors could interpret multiple leads simultaneously, cross-reference past recordings, and act on AI suggestions without losing their train of thought.

Existing tools buried key information in menus, lacked real algorithm suggestions, and made serial comparisons nearly impossible. The ECG Analysis Terminal needed to surface the right information at the right moment in a single, distraction-free workspace.

My Approach

How I Approached It

I started by finding where diagnosis time actually went. Research showed most of it was lost switching between panels rather than on interpretation, so the strategy became: keep everything a doctor needs in one view.

Information architecture came before any UI. The panel layout was validated with doctors through card sorting so the workspace matched how they already think.

Vision & Innovation

The Vision

A single, distraction-free clinical workspace where doctors can read multiple leads, compare against past ECGs and act on AI suggestions without losing their train of thought. The aim was diagnosis in seconds, not minutes.

Identifying Unique Challenges

What Made It Hard

  • Buried information: Key data sat behind menus, forcing constant navigation during a read.
  • Context switching: Most diagnosis time was lost moving between tool panels rather than interpreting.
  • Serial comparison: Comparing a current ECG with previous ones was nearly impossible in existing tools.
  • Clinical environments: Screens are read at a distance, under hospital lighting, over long shifts.
Resolving Complex Problems

Design Solutions

3-panel workspace

Queue, viewer and analysis live side by side, so doctors never leave the read to find information.

Layout validated with 8 doctors in card sorting, with no confusion on spatial layout.

Inline AI suggestions

Algorithmic suggestions appear in the analysis panel next to the waveform, ready to accept or adjust.

Contributed to an 85% faster diagnosis workflow than the legacy tool.

Side-by-side serial comparison

Current and previous ECGs can be compared directly. The entry point was repositioned using Hotjar heatmaps.

Serial comparison click-through improved from 12% to 68%.

Distance-tested type scale

Typography tuned for clinical monitors viewed from 60 to 90 cm in day and night brightness.

Passed legibility tests at 90 cm on standard hospital monitors.

Design Process

Designing the Workspace

01 — Research

Heuristic evaluation of 5 existing ECG platforms. 3-week diary study with cardiologists to understand real web workflows and context switching patterns.

Identified that 70% of diagnosis time was lost switching between tool panels — not on interpretation itself.

02 — Information Architecture

Defined a 3-panel layout: queue, viewer, and analysis. Card sorting with doctors confirmed the mental model and panel hierarchy before any UI was drawn.

3-panel layout validated with 8 doctors in card sorting sessions — zero confusion on spatial layout.

03 — Design

Designed 40+ screens across 12 flows. Iterated on typography scale for legibility at clinical monitor distances (60–90 cm). Every element tested under real hospital ambient light conditions.

Final type scale passed legibility tests at 90 cm on standard hospital monitors at both day and night brightness.

04 — Validate

Deployed Hotjar recordings on beta. Identified heat patterns showing doctors weren't seeing the serial comparison CTA — repositioned and retested until click-through reached target.

Serial comparison CTA click-through improved from 12% to 68% after two repositioning iterations.
Accessibility & Optimisation

Built for Everyone

  • Readable at distance: type scale tested at 90 cm on standard hospital monitors.
  • Lighting tested: verified under real hospital ambient light at both day and night brightness.
  • Clear hierarchy: critical information surfaced first, with fewer clicks to reach it.
  • Data-driven optimisation: Hotjar heatmaps used to find and fix overlooked interactions.
